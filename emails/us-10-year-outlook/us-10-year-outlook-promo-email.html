Growth, AI investment, inflation, rates and fiscal risks: our panel's view to 2035.

What does the next decade hold for the U.S. economy?
 
Access Special Report

U.S. to keep outpacing its peers: Our Consensus is for the U.S. economy to continue growing significantly faster than the G7 average over the next ten years, despite near-term headwinds from tariffs and tighter immigration policy.

AI investment will be an important driver: Spending on data centers and related AI infrastructure is set to stay strong in the coming years, bar any major speed bumps, alongside productivity gains and resilient consumer spending. But our panelists are far from unanimous on how big the AI boost will be.

Fiscal risks will grow more pressing: With the federal deficit forecast to stay near 6% of GDP, about double the G7 average, public debt is set to climb to a multi-decade high as a share of the economy. Investor jitters are already showing up in government borrowing costs.
 

Insight from our panelists:

Goldman Sachs’ Eric Sheridan on the AI capex cycle:

“Our initial take is that the capex cycle through the end of 2027 is likely to remain elevated and in line with our estimates, which are higher than the Wall Street consensus. My colleagues and I estimate that US hyperscalers will deploy $1.4 trillion in capital in 2027. There are constraints up and down the supply chain, including access to memory chips and issues relating to power and land.”
